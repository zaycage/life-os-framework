# Less Friction

> Removing friction is often easier than increasing motivation.

---

# What

Friction is anything that consumes energy without creating meaningful value.

It is the denominator of the Success Equation.

Every unit of friction reduces the effectiveness of your systems, your consistency, and your time.

---

# Why

Most people try to overpower friction with willpower.

Willpower is expensive and unreliable.

Friction removal is cheap and permanent.

A system with less friction requires less motivation to run.

The goal is not to try harder.

The goal is to make the right action the easy action.

---

# Types of Friction

## Physical

Anything that reduces the body's capacity.

Examples

- Poor sleep
- Chronic pain
- Fatigue
- Neglected health

---

## Mental

Anything that consumes cognitive or emotional energy without resolution.

Examples

- Decision fatigue
- Rumination
- Anxiety
- Carrying unresolved thoughts

---

## Environmental

Anything in your surroundings that resists the right action.

Examples

- Clutter
- Disorganization
- Broken tools
- Inefficient workspace

---

## Technological

Anything in your tools that interrupts or slows you.

Examples

- Slow devices
- Broken workflows
- Notification overload

---

## Financial

Anything that creates ongoing monetary drag or uncertainty.

Examples

- Debt
- Poor budgeting
- Unnecessary subscriptions

---

## Relational

Anything unresolved between people.

Examples

- Poor communication
- Avoided conversations
- Unspoken expectations

---

## Time

Anything that repeatedly consumes time without creating value.

Examples

- Long commutes
- Repeated manual work
- Constant interruptions

---

## Productive Avoidance

The most dangerous type of friction, because it disguises itself as progress.

Productive Avoidance is choosing low-friction substitutes for high-friction action.

Examples

- Reading about improvement instead of practicing it
- Planning systems instead of running them
- Refining tools instead of doing the work
- Consuming content about your goals instead of pursuing them
- Reorganizing instead of executing

Test

> Am I doing this because it moves the work forward, or because it feels like the work without the cost?

---

# Principles

1. Remove friction before adding effort.

2. Recurring friction deserves permanent solutions.

3. Friction reveals where the next system should be built.

4. The absence of a system is itself a form of friction.

5. Beware friction that looks like progress.

---

# Applications

## Diagnose

Keep a running list of recurring friction.

If something frustrates you more than twice, write it down.

---

## Prioritize

Rank friction by how often it recurs and how much energy it consumes.

Daily friction outranks occasional friction.

---

## Remove

For each friction, ask:

- Can I eliminate it entirely?
- Can I automate it?
- Can I build a system that prevents it from recurring?
- Can I reduce how often I face it?

---

## Review

Friction accumulates silently.

Review your friction list on a regular cadence.

Removing friction is not a project.

It is a practice.
