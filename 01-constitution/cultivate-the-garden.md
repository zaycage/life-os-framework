# Cultivate the Garden

> You do not control outcomes. You create conditions.

---

# What

The Success Equation describes the parts of life that work like machines.

Inputs, levers, outputs.

But the most important parts of life do not work like machines.

Health, relationships, character, grief, children, mastery — these work like gardens.

A garden cannot be forced.

It can only be tended.

Cultivating the garden means creating the conditions for growth, then trusting the growth to happen on its own schedule.

---

# Why

Machine thinking applied to garden problems creates suffering.

You cannot optimize a marriage into flourishing.

You cannot force a child to grow on schedule.

You cannot debug grief.

Treating these as engineering problems leads to frustration when effort does not produce immediate output.

Garden thinking restores the correct expectation:

Faithful tending, patient waiting, seasonal change.

The equation and the garden are both true.

Wisdom is knowing which one you are standing in.

---

# The Practices

## Watering

Small, consistent care.

Most living things die from neglect, not catastrophe.

Question

> What am I quietly neglecting?

---

## Weeding

Some friction returns no matter how well you remove it.

Weeds are not a sign of failure.

They are a sign the garden is alive.

Weeding is not a project.

It is a recurring practice.

Question

> What needs pulling again this week?

---

## Pruning

Growth requires removal.

Sometimes good things must be cut so better things can flourish.

Pruning is the hardest practice because it removes what is alive and healthy — for the sake of what matters more.

Question

> What good thing is crowding out a better thing?

---

## Planting

Some seasons are for starting things whose harvest is years away.

Plant anyway.

Question

> What am I planting today that Future Me will harvest?

---

## Seasons

Gardens do not grow year-round.

There are seasons of growth, seasons of harvest, and seasons where the only work is survival.

Winter is not failure.

Winter is part of the cycle.

Question

> What season am I actually in?

---

# Principles

1. Create conditions. Release outcomes.

2. Tend small and often rather than heroically and rarely.

3. Weeds return. Pull them anyway.

4. Prune good things to protect better things.

5. Respect the season you are in.

6. Tend your own garden before commenting on anyone else's.

---

# Applications

## Relationships

Presence, attention, and small consistent care outperform grand gestures.

You cannot make someone flourish.

You can make it easier for them to.

---

## Health

The body is grown, not built.

Consistency of inputs, patience with results.

---

## Grief and Hardship

Some things are not friction to remove.

They are seasons to move through.

Tend what can be tended.

Endure what must be endured.

---

## Work and Mastery

Skills compound like perennials.

Years of quiet tending precede visible bloom.
